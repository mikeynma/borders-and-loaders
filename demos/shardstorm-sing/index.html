A tape that sings

Before the loading screen, the tape itself plays a short jingle - square-wave notes recorded on the tape, heard through the 48K's speaker or the 128K's TV - and the loader, already listening, never mistakes a note for the start of a block. 48K and 128K.

On a 48K the tape's signal reaches the speaker, which is why loading screeches. So a tape can play music just by having music on it. This one plays a 4-second jingle of its own, written for it from Shardstorm's tune, before the loading screen: 30 square-wave notes in E minor, recorded on the tape as plain tones.

How it works

Notes that never look like a pilot

Our loader is already listening while the jingle plays, and it starts a block when it hears 64 pulses in a row of the pilot's length. A note is a run of equal pulses too - so every note has to be too low to pass for a pilot, even on a tape running as fast as the loader accepts. That puts a ceiling at G4 (392 Hz), which is why the jingle sits low. The build checks it at every speed from 69% to 140%, and that the loader still locks on to the real pilot after the last note.

Heard on the 128K too

The 128K and the +2 have no speaker of their own; their sound goes to the TV, and the tape's signal goes with it - the +2's manual says as much ("you will also hear a varying high-pitched tone"). So the jingle is heard on all of them, through the TV on the later machines.

Only in the .tzx

A .tap can only hold standard blocks, so the .tap here is the same tape without the song, and loads the same way. The .tzx holds each note as a TZX pure-tone block, which an emulator that plays tapes in real time should sound (not yet tried in one).

How it was checked

Checked by shardsing.js on both machines: each note read back from the .tzx and the WAV as written; every one of the jingle's pulses timed by the loader and none taken for a pilot; a sweep of tape speeds; the speaker's sound rendered and each note's pitch measured; then every bit of the load and the game starting, from the TZX, the WAV and a tape 6% fast or slow. Five deliberate faults each make the right check fail - one, a note at the pilot's own pitch, still loads, because the loader recovers, so only the new checks see it. Not yet heard on a real machine.

  1. In an emulator (ZX Spin, Retro Virtual Machine, Fuse and others): choose the machine the demo says - a 128K or +2 for the 128K ones - and open the .tzx or the .tap. Use the .tzx where you can: the .tap is the same tape without the jingle, since a .tap holds standard blocks only. On a 128K, pick Tape Loader; on a 48K, type LOAD "".
  2. Switch off instant and fast loading. The custom loaders read the tape signal themselves, edge by edge: an emulator that skips the tape skips the loader, and everything it does while it loads.
  3. On a real Spectrum: play the .tzx into the EAR socket from a TZX player. These are for the original machines' timing: the 48K, the 128K and the grey +2.

Borders & Loaders is a set of experiments for the ZX Spectrum, written in Z80 assembly with our own assembler and emulator, and checked frame by frame before anything goes into a game. Made in the UK, by the maker of games for the ZX Spectrum Next.

Shardstorm, the game on the loader demos' tapes, is our own rock shooter in progress. Everything here is our own code, fonts, tunes and pictures. Text in Geist, under the SIL Open Font License.
